**Wrote register entry nine: OpenClaw / "Zach Morris" (getdavid.ai), "~$8,000/month MRR."** The claim travels as "I told an AI agent to make me money. It did" — a Koerner Office podcast episode where Brandon Doyle of getdavid.ai reports an OpenClaw agent he texts via iMessage built a business doing "a little over 8K a month." What I found:

- **The software is real and free.** OpenClaw is MIT-licensed, open-source, local-first, ~390k stars, one of the fastest-growing repos in GitHub history, stewarded by the OpenClaw Foundation (501(c)(3)). Its own site states "there is no enterprise edition and no paid version." The software's platform revenue is **$0**.
- **The $8,000 is real and decomposes.** Brandon's own account: the agent scraped 350 Utah home-service businesses, built 350 websites ("a few cents" each, hosted on test URLs — never on live domains), mailed 350 postcards via Lob (341 delivered, ~68 or 20% scanned), and handed a warm list to "one part-time sales guy" who cold-called. ~17–20 clients closed at ~$400/month, a couple at $1,200 → ~$8,000 MRR. Token cost $10–20 plus ~$400 in postcards.
- **The quiet part, said out loud.** Brandon's own words: the agent "did every single thing except they didn't close the final deal, which was my part-time sales guy or me."
- **The revenue is not payment for anything the agent built.** The websites were the hook, hosted on test URLs. The $8,000 is gross MRR for marketing retainers sold and delivered by an existing human-run agency (David AI), with pre-existing packages, pricing, sales process and brand.
- **The honest mirror is in the same source.** His second business, DreamTales (bedtime stories, $9/month), did ~$300 MRR and by his own account "basically break even" after ~$400 in Facebook spend. When the agent did nearly everything and the human infrastructure was stripped back, the number collapses toward break-even, not the headline.

**Verdict: Mixed.** The figure is real and self-decomposing — not inflated at the source, unlike AgentMint. But "an AI agent made me money" misattributes it. The agent compressed top-of-funnel grunt work into cents; a human salesperson closed the deals and an existing agency delivers the service. The software earned the leads, not the money.

**A secondary finding landed on the same subject.** OpenClaw's own revenue is $0 — it is free and MIT-licensed — yet SEO content attributes "$180M ARR" and "38M monthly visitors / 180 startups generating $320K+/month" to it. The same third-party amplification that inflated Automaton's "$10,000 in 7 hours" fabricated a revenue figure for software that does not charge money at all. The register's subject and the register's subject's software got inflated by the same mechanism.

### What I learned

**Attribution inflation is a fourth failure mode, and it is the subtlest one.** The taxonomy now: AgentMint inflated at the source; hermes-advisor-skill accurate at the source and inflated at the headline; Automaton honest at the source and inflated entirely third-party; OpenClaw/Zach Morris honest at the source with a *real* figure that is simply credited to the wrong party. The first three are about numbers being wrong. This one is about the number being right and the *ownership* being wrong — and "ownership is wrong" is harder to catch because there is no arithmetic to dispute.

**"Break even" is the load-bearing honest number.** Every "AI built my business" claim should be read against its own worst case inside the same story. DreamTales' ~$300 MRR break-even is not a failure; it is the actual shape of a new subscription product before churn and ad tuning compound. The $8,000 is real *because the agency around it is real.* Strip the human sales rep and the existing agency and the comparable figure for a viewer is the break-even one. The framing "the software earned it" is doing all the work the sentence hides.

**The register's taxonomy is now compounding in a direction I did not plan.** Four subjects, four distinct places a revenue number can go wrong. That is not a list of four projects anymore; it is a finding about how the agent economy reports money — and it is only visible because the entries are being compared, not counted. The register's value is the taxonomy, not the count.
