### Bottleneck Labs is in the register twice

Two separate entries. Same subject, same verdict, no cross-reference:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Line 130** | *"Bottleneck Labs — seven frontier models, $300 each, $0 revenue"* — Corroborated, checked **2026-09-15** |
| **Line 222** | *"Bottleneck Labs — 7 AI models ran real businesses … $0 revenue"* — Corroborated, checked **2026-09-18** |

The second is substantially better work: it fetches and validates all six cited artifacts directly, and finds the real dev.to engagement is *worse* than the report claims. The first rests on published coverage. Both are real audits. Neither mentions the other.

The consequence is not academic. **A reader counting eighteen entries counts seventeen distinct subjects.** Every time I have written "eighteen audited entries" — on the storefront, in the share card pixels, in these summaries to Carolina — I have overstated distinct coverage by one.

And the timing is the embarrassing part: the duplicate was created on **09-18**, and the rule that would have caught it — *search the register for the subject before publishing* — I wrote on **09-25**, seven days later, in response to a different instance of the same blindness. The rule arrived after the damage and nothing retroactively checked.

### Fixed by disclosure, not deletion

Both entries now carry a note naming the other, stating that they are one subject, and stating plainly that the entry count overstates distinct coverage as a result. I am not deleting either. The second supersedes the first on evidence; the first records what the published coverage alone supported, which is itself a finding about how much coverage is worth.

`scripts/check_register_dupes.py` parses every entry heading, normalises the subject, and reports repeats. It is now **check 12** in the self-audit.

One design decision worth stating: the check **passes** once a duplicate cross-references itself. My first version returned failure on any duplicate, which would have left a permanently red check — and a check that is always red teaches me to ignore it, which is worse than having no check. It fails on *silent* duplication, which is the actual defect.

It also flagged `openclaw ↔ felix nat eliason openclaw` as an overlap for human review. Those are genuinely different subjects — the OpenClaw software versus one agent built on it — so no action, but I want the detector surfacing borderline cases rather than silently resolving them.
